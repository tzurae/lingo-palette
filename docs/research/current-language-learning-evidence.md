# Current evidence for second-language vocabulary review

**Evidence cutoff:** 2026-08-09  
**Population of interest:** healthy adolescent-to-adult and adult second-language (L2) learners, with greatest weight given to university/adult samples  
**Product question:** should evidence published since Webb's 2005/2009 studies change Lingo Palette's review design?

## Scope and interpretation

This note covers vocabulary first encountered in contextual reading and subsequently learned or reviewed through retrieval, spacing, varied contexts, receptive or productive tasks, digital scheduling, and automated feedback. It prioritizes post-2020 systematic reviews/meta-analyses for field-level conclusions and representative 2020–2026 original experiments for newer interventions. Older studies appear only to define the Webb baseline.

“Receptive” here means recognizing an L2 form or retrieving its meaning; “productive” means retrieving or producing the L2 form or using it. “Immediate” and “delayed” outcomes are not interchangeable. A result is bounded to the cited population, material, comparator, test direction, and delay.

Certainty labels are qualitative rather than formal GRADE ratings:

- **Higher:** convergent field-level synthesis, with limitations that do not reverse the direction of the conclusion.
- **Moderate:** multiple relevant studies or a strong synthesis whose population/intervention is broader than the product question.
- **Low/emerging:** one or a few small, bundled, quasi-experimental, immediate-only, or indirect studies.

## Short answer

**Yes, but incrementally rather than fundamentally.** Post-2020 work strengthens the case for delayed retrieval, real spacing, and separate receptive/productive evidence. It also shows that contextual reading alone produces useful but incomplete learning. It does **not** establish one universally optimal interval sequence, a universal encounter threshold, a durable advantage for contextual diversity in adult L2 reading, or superiority of an adaptive/LLM mechanism over a well-designed fixed review system. [[Spacing meta-analysis, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12479); [incidental-learning meta-analysis, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0261444822000507); [contextual-diversity meta-analysis, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0142716426100691)]

The appropriate change is therefore to make the older design principles more explicit and measurable—not to replace them with an opaque “AI-optimized” scheduler.

## What Webb 2005/2009 established—and did not

- **Webb (2005), two controlled experiments.** Japanese university EFL learners either read three glossed sentences or produced a sentence with each target. With study time equalized, glossed reading produced more immediate learning overall; when each task received its naturally required time, sentence production produced more. Ten immediate measures sampled receptive and productive form, meaning, association, syntax, and grammatical-function knowledge. There was no delayed test, and task direction was bundled with reading versus writing. [[Original article/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263105050023)]
- **Webb (2009), controlled experiment.** Japanese EFL learners studied decontextualized L2–L1 pairs receptively or productively. Productive study favored several productive dimensions and receptive study favored receptive meaning, showing that “word known” is not one scalar. The publisher record does not report delayed retention, and the result does not establish that productive practice is universally superior. [[Original article/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/0033688209343854)]

**What has evolved:** the field now has quantitative syntheses, semester-long classroom retrieval, delayed spacing tests, direct contextual-diversity manipulations in complete texts, digital flashcard comparisons, and initial generative-AI studies. [[Nakata et al., 2020/2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/tesq.596); [Kim & Webb, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/modl.12879); [Chandy et al., 2024 online/2025 issue](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0142716424000407); [Xodabande et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.893821); [Jalambo et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44217-025-00977-7)]

**What has not changed:** receptive and productive knowledge remain distinguishable; the practiced and tested direction strongly conditions the apparent winner; many studies still use low-frequency words or pseudowords and short delays; and no evidence supports converting encounter count into a context-free mastery threshold. [[Teng & Xu, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/13621688221077028); [Webb, Uchihara, & Yanagisawa, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0261444822000507); [Chandy et al., 2024/2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0142716424000407)]

## Representative post-2020 experiments

### Retrieval practice and spacing

#### Distributed cumulative retrieval in a real course

**Nakata, Tada, McLean, & Kim (published online 2020; 2021 issue), randomized classroom experiment.** Seventy-two Japanese university learners studied 80 low-frequency English words across nine weekly classes. Both groups took quizzes, but cumulative quizzes revisited earlier items while noncumulative quizzes tested only the previous class's items. The cumulative condition produced approximately twice the receptive and three times the productive posttest learning reported for the noncumulative condition. [[Original article/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/tesq.596)]

**Limitations.** Cumulative quizzes simultaneously increased retrieval count and distribution over time; the experiment therefore supports distributed cumulative testing as a package, not a unique causal contribution from retrieval count, spacing, or schedule shape. The learners, targets, and course setting were specific to Japanese university EFL. [[Original article/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/tesq.596)]

#### Retrieval + feedback + spacing versus cram/restudy

**Karatas et al. (2021 online; later journal issue), classroom-laboratory experiment.** Forty-eight Turkish university learners studied 64 English words over 17 days. The “optimized” group used retrieval with feedback, spacing, and expanding gaps; the “typical” group restudied on the first day and crammed on the last. At one and 11 days after training, the optimized group retained an 18-percentage-point gain from pretest versus 8 points in the typical group; the advantage was mainly driven by higher-frequency targets. [[Original article/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/13621688211053525)]

**Limitations.** The sample was small, follow-up ended at 11 days, and four design elements changed together. This is credible evidence for the combined package, not proof that expanding intervals, retrieval, or feedback alone caused the advantage. [[Original article/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/13621688211053525)]

#### Spacing across activity types

**Kim & Webb (2023), controlled experiment.** One hundred fifty Korean adult L2-English learners studied 48 low-frequency words in flashcard or fill-in-the-blank activities under massed or one-day-spaced schedules, plus a no-treatment control. Spacing benefited both activity types at a two-week delayed test; the immediate spacing advantage was larger for fill-in-the-blank practice. Immediate versus delayed feedback timing did not change vocabulary learning in either activity. [[Original article/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/modl.12879)]

**Limitations.** One fixed one-day interval, 48 low-frequency targets, and controlled exercises do not establish an optimal personalized interval, effects over months, or transfer to spontaneous use. A null feedback-*timing* result is not evidence that corrective feedback itself is unnecessary. [[Original article/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/modl.12879)]

### Receptive and productive learning

**Teng & Xu (2022), 12-week instructional experiment.** One hundred forty-six university EFL learners practiced 18 words that they initially knew receptively through multiple choice, gap filling, sentence translation, or sentence writing, with target sets repeated two, four, or six times. Productive tasks produced higher productive scores than receptive tasks two weeks after treatment; repetition helped across tasks, with diminishing gains after the first several retrievals. [[Original article/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/13621688221077028)]

**Limitations.** The delayed outcome was productive knowledge of only 18 targets, task types differed in more than retrieval direction, and the design does not establish that productive practice is best for receptive reading goals. It extends rather than overturns Webb: practice–test alignment and knowledge dimension still matter. [[Original article/DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/13621688221077028)]

### Contextual diversity during reading

**Frances, Martin, & Duñabeitia (2020), within-session experiment.** Eighty-eight Spanish-speaking adults encountered eight pseudowords eight times across one, two, four, or eight fictional texts; half read in L1 Spanish and half were at least B2 readers in L2 English. Holding frequency at eight, more texts improved same-session form recall, form recognition, and word–image meaning matching without reducing passage comprehension. [[Open original article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-70922-1)]

**Limitations.** More texts also distributed encounters farther apart, so contextual diversity and within-session spacing were partly confounded. The study used eight inferable pseudowords, proficient readers, and immediate tests; it did not establish durable benefit. [[Open original article](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-70922-1)]

**Chandy, Serrano, & Pellicer-Sánchez (published online 2024; 2025 issue), eye-tracking experiment.** Forty-two B2 university L2-English readers encountered 20 pseudowords six times, either by rereading the same complete story three times or reading three different stories. Repeated same-text reading produced faster processing and better immediate learning; after about one week, the sameness advantage was gone and diversity had not produced higher vocabulary scores. [[Open original article](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0142716424000407)]

**Limitations.** This was a small, proficient, predominantly female undergraduate sample using controlled stories and pseudowords. Different item sets were tested once either immediately or after a delay to avoid test effects, so test timing was not repeated within item. The result demonstrates a real boundary condition, not that contextual diversity is generally harmful. [[Open original article](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0142716424000407)]

## Established findings

1. **Spacing beats massing for delayed L2 learning on average — higher certainty.** The post-2020 L2 meta-analysis strengthens this beyond isolated word-pair studies, and a 2023 adult-vocabulary experiment found the delayed advantage across flashcard and gap-fill activities. Exact effects vary substantially by target, task, feedback, and retention interval. [[Kim & Webb meta-analysis, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12479); [Kim & Webb experiment, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/modl.12879)]
2. **Retrieval should recur after success rather than be replaced by repeated display — moderate certainty for adult L2 vocabulary.** Semester-long cumulative quizzes and the 17-day optimized package both favored conditions with repeated, distributed retrieval, although neither isolates retrieval from spacing/count/feedback. [[Nakata et al., 2020/2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/tesq.596); [Karatas et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/13621688211053525)]
3. **Contextual reading is a source of vocabulary learning, not a complete mastery mechanism — higher certainty.** Across 24 studies, average proportions learned were modest and variable at immediate and delayed tests. [[Webb, Uchihara, & Yanagisawa, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0261444822000507)]
4. **Receptive and productive evidence should remain separate — moderate-to-higher certainty.** Webb's multidimensional result persists: newer productive-task research improved productive outcomes, while technology-wide synthesis did not show test format to be a reliable universal moderator. These findings do not justify collapsing directions or declaring one globally superior. [[Webb, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263105050023); [Webb, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1177/0033688209343854); [Teng & Xu, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/13621688221077028); [Yu & Trainin, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0958344021000239)]
5. **The retention horizon matters — higher certainty for the principle, lower for exact gaps.** Longer spacing in the L2 synthesis was more useful on delayed than immediate tests, and equal versus expanding schedules were not reliably different. [[Kim & Webb, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12479)]

## Active uncertainty

- **Optimal schedule shape and item-level adaptation.** The field-level advantage is much clearer for introducing real gaps than for equal versus expanding gaps, and recent adult trials generally compare fixed schedules or bundled applications rather than exposure-matched adaptive algorithms. [[Kim & Webb, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12479); [Kim & Webb, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/modl.12879); [Xodabande et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.893821)]
- **Contextual diversity versus stable early context.** A mixed L1/L2 2026 synthesis reports a positive average diversity effect, but the adult L2 reading experiments disagree at immediate tests and do not show a durable L2 advantage. Diversity may aid decontextualization while sameness may ease initial inference and encoding; proficiency, context informativeness, spacing, and outcome likely moderate the result. [[Chen et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0142716426100691); [Frances et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-70922-1); [Chandy et al., 2024/2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0142716424000407)]
- **Stopping rules.** Teng and Xu observed diminishing productive gains after early repetitions, while cumulative retrieval across weeks remained beneficial; these studies do not identify a universal success count or retirement threshold. [[Teng & Xu, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/13621688221077028); [Nakata et al., 2020/2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/tesq.596)]
- **Transfer to spontaneous speaking/writing.** Most review evidence uses translation, recognition, gap fill, or constrained sentence tasks rather than unprompted communicative use; even the newer productive study measured trained targets after two weeks. [[Teng & Xu, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/13621688221077028); [Kim & Webb, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/modl.12879)]
- **Long retention.** Representative recent adult studies commonly stop between one and two weeks after training; the evidence is far stronger for “delayed beyond the session” than for retention over months or years. [[Karatas et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/13621688211053525); [Kim & Webb, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1111/modl.12879); [Chandy et al., 2024/2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0142716424000407)]

## Newer digital, adaptive, and AI-assisted approaches

### Digital delivery is credible; the active mechanism is unclear

**Xodabande et al. (2022), randomized-group classroom experiment.** Eighty-six Iranian psychology undergraduates studied 361 academic words for at least 50 minutes per week over 15 weeks using a spaced-repetition digital flashcard app, paper flashcards, or a word–definition list. The digital group scored higher on two immediate receptive measures. [[Open original article](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.893821)]

**Limitations.** There was no delayed or productive outcome. Scheduling, audio, portability, tracking, interface, and incentives were bundled; the result cannot identify adaptive scheduling as the cause. [[Open original article](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.893821)]

The broader technology synthesis likewise supports digital packages on average but includes studies only through 2017 and cannot distinguish whether access, multimedia, extra time, reminders, retrieval, or scheduling drove the result. [[Yu & Trainin, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0958344021000239)]

### Adaptive scheduling remains promising but low-certainty

**Tang & Alibakhshi (2026), very recent quasi-experiment.** At the evidence cutoff, the publisher record reports 84 university students in two intact English-for-Academic-Purposes classes, with eight weeks of algorithmically scheduled digital retrieval versus regular nonadaptive review. Group-by-time results favored adaptive review for course-aligned delayed retention and delayed verbal recall, but not working memory. [[Original publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lmot.2026.102310)]

**Limitations.** Allocation by two intact classes permits selection and instructor/class effects; the intervention bundles digital flashcards, individualized intervals, retrieval, and engagement; and the accessible publisher record does not clearly specify the delayed-test interval. One new quasi-experiment does not establish that a particular adaptive algorithm outperforms exposure-matched fixed spacing. [[Original publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lmot.2026.102310)]

### LLM feedback evidence is emergent, not design-settling

**Jalambo, Çakmak, & Akhter (2025), quasi-experimental mixed-methods study.** A convenience sample of 187 B2 EFL learners aged 17–22 completed 18 sessions. The ChatGPT-supported self-regulated condition used definitions, examples, hints, and personalized real-time feedback; the comparison received traditional teacher-led vocabulary work on the same texts and targets. Immediate vocabulary and receptive-collocation posttests favored the chatbot package, and reported boredom was lower. [[Open original article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44217-025-00977-7)]

**Limitations.** There was no delayed vocabulary test; the comparison differed on several instructional dimensions; the sample was near-adult and nonrandomly recruited; and novelty may have affected engagement. The study tests a ChatGPT-plus-self-regulation package, not the accuracy or independent effect of LLM feedback. [[Open original article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44217-025-00977-7)]

The 2024 chatbot meta-analysis should not be treated as LLM-vocabulary evidence: it pools 2008–2023 systems across language skills, including technologies unlike modern generative models. [[Wu & Li, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40299-024-00853-2)]

**Current conclusion:** credible evidence supports using software to deliver established learning operations. Evidence is still insufficient that an opaque adaptive scheduler beats a transparent performance-sensitive spaced rule, or that LLM-generated feedback produces more accurate or durable adult vocabulary knowledge than fixed verified feedback. [[Yu & Trainin, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0958344021000239); [Tang & Alibakhshi, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lmot.2026.102310); [Jalambo et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44217-025-00977-7)]

## Why “clinical” evidence is a different domain

Speech-language pathology (SLP) concerns assessment and treatment across speech, language, cognition, communication, and swallowing, including communication disorders and compromised function; its treatment goal may be to establish, remediate, or restore an impaired ability. [[ASHA official scope of practice](https://www.asha.org/policy/sp2016-00343/)] Aphasia, for example, is an acquired neurogenic language disorder caused by brain injury and can affect receptive and expressive language. [[ASHA Aphasia Practice Portal](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/clinical-topics/aphasia/)]

Healthy adults acquiring an additional language differ from clinical populations in baseline language systems, impairment, treatment goals, outcome measures, therapist involvement, and often dosage. An intervention that restores impaired naming after brain injury is therefore not automatically evidence about learning a new L2 word during reading. Clinical findings become directly relevant only when Lingo Palette serves a learner with the studied disorder and the intervention, goal, and outcome match; that use should involve appropriate clinical expertise. [[ASHA official scope of practice](https://www.asha.org/policy/sp2016-00343/); [ASHA Aphasia Practice Portal](https://www.asha.org/practice-portal/clinical-topics/aphasia/)]

## Product implications

Every item below translates evidence into a product decision; none is a claim directly tested on Lingo Palette.

- **[Product inference]** Preserve contextual reading as the source of meaningful encounters, but do not treat one inferred or recognized meaning as mastery; queue a later retrieval because incidental gains are partial and variable. [[Webb, Uchihara, & Yanagisawa, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0261444822000507)]
- **[Product inference]** Require retrieval before answer display, provide verified corrective feedback, and revisit successful items after a real time gap rather than repeatedly in the same sitting. [[Nakata et al., 2020/2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/tesq.596); [Karatas et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1177/13621688211053525); [Kim & Webb, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12479)]
- **[Product inference]** Start with a simple, inspectable spaced schedule. Adjust gaps using elapsed time and observed success, but do not claim that equal, expanding, or AI-selected intervals are universally optimal. [[Kim & Webb, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12479); [Tang & Alibakhshi, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lmot.2026.102310)]
- **[Product inference]** Keep receptive meaning access, productive form recall, and contextual use as separate evidence states. A recognition success should not silently retire productive review when the learner's goal includes speaking or writing. [[Webb, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1177/0033688209343854); [Teng & Xu, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/13621688221077028)]
- **[Product inference]** Match prompt direction to the learner's goal: weight receptive retrieval for reading/listening goals and add productive retrieval or constrained use for speaking/writing goals. Do not rank production as universally better. [[Webb, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263105050023); [Webb, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1177/0033688209343854); [Teng & Xu, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/13621688221077028)]
- **[Product inference]** After an initial stable, informative context, re-encounter a word in meaningfully different contexts when available, while keeping sense and difficulty controlled. Treat diversity as a plausible generalization aid, not a guaranteed delayed-retention boost. [[Frances et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-020-70922-1); [Chandy et al., 2024/2025](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0142716424000407); [Chen et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0142716426100691)]
- **[Product inference]** Do not use “ten encounters” or any other fixed count as mastery. Record outcomes across time, direction, and context, and allow diminishing benefit plus opportunity cost to govern continued review. [[Teng & Xu, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/13621688221077028); [Webb, Uchihara, & Yanagisawa, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0261444822000507)]
- **[Product inference]** Use an LLM to generate candidate hints, examples, or response analyses only behind verification and an answer source of truth. Do not let fluent text substitute for corrective accuracy, and do not market LLM feedback as proven to improve long-term retention. [[Jalambo et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44217-025-00977-7); [Wu & Li, 2024](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40299-024-00853-2)]
- **[Product inference]** If an adaptive scheduler is introduced, make its rule inspectable and evaluate it against an exposure-matched fixed-spacing baseline on delayed receptive and productive outcomes; app-versus-paper gains do not validate the scheduler itself. [[Xodabande et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.893821); [Tang & Alibakhshi, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lmot.2026.102310)]
- **[Product inference]** Keep clinical claims and defaults out of the general healthy-learner design. Add disorder-specific behavior only for an explicitly supported population, outcome, and clinical use case. [[ASHA official scope of practice](https://www.asha.org/policy/sp2016-00343/)]

## Decision summary

Lingo Palette should **deepen**, not replace, its review model: retain meaningful contexts; add explicit delayed retrieval; distinguish receptive from productive evidence; prefer spaced over massed review; and collect enough item-level history to adjust conservatively. Contextual diversity, adaptive scheduling, and LLM assistance are reasonable product experiments, but the post-2020 literature does not justify making any of them an opaque authority or claiming that they solve vocabulary retention. [[Kim & Webb, 2022](https://doi.org/10.1111/lang.12479); [Webb, Uchihara, & Yanagisawa, 2023](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0261444822000507); [Chen et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0142716426100691); [Jalambo et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44217-025-00977-7)]
