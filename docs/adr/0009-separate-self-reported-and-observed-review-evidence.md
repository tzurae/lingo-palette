# Separate self-reported and observed review evidence

Review remains lightweight by combining covert recall followed by learner-rated recall with periodic objectively answered calibration items, while productive review always requires an overt response. The system records self-assessment and scored responses as distinct review evidence by knowledge dimension and response method, and may reduce scheduling reliance on self-assessment when it repeatedly conflicts with observed performance. Overt responses receive a review judgment of demonstrated, acceptable alternative, partial, not demonstrated, or unable to grade: only demonstrated is positive evidence for the target dimension, acceptable alternatives are not mislabeled as errors, and unable-to-grade results carry no scheduling penalty. This preserves low-friction sessions without pretending every confidence tap measures demonstrated knowledge or every valid expression proves recall of the target.

Covert recall uses a three-level Retrieval Fluency self-assessment: `did-not-recall`, `recalled-with-effort`, or `recalled-fluently`. It is stored as self-assessed Review Evidence, not converted into an objective Review Judgment. Its schedule transitions and calibration gates are explicit separate decisions.

Calibration is tracked per Knowledge Dimension. Its first review is objectively scored; after three self-assessed reviews, the next review is objective. An objective item records Retrieval Fluency before revealing its result, pairing it with the Review Judgment. A mismatch is `recalled-fluently` with any gradable judgment other than demonstrated, or `did-not-recall` with demonstrated. Two mismatches among the latest three gradable calibration pairs put that dimension into objective-only mode. Two consecutive non-mismatching pairs restore the three-to-one cadence. Unable-to-grade evidence neither creates nor clears mismatch.
